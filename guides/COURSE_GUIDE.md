# Teaching microeconomics through Marginal Falls

The town supplies continuity across the course. Its scarce riverfront, investigation budget, bakery, market, bridge, coffee shop, and shuttle operators make new concepts part of a developing economic setting.

## The teaching sequence

1. **Act I — Building the Stage:** introduce scarcity, opportunity cost, incentives, marginal decisions, and trade. Students first learn how economic reasoning changes a town's decisions.
2. **Act II — Casting the Buyers:** develop utility and budget constraints before deriving demand and measuring its sensitivity to price.
3. **Act III — Casting the Sellers:** develop technology, production, and costs before examining profit-maximizing output, supply, and supply elasticity.
4. **Act IV — The Play Begins:** combine buyers and sellers in equilibrium, assess gains from exchange and policy, introduce externalities and public goods, and connect output markets to hiring.
5. **Act V — Different Stages:** compare perfect competition, monopoly, monopolistic competition, and oligopoly. The setting changes how much market power a seller has and whether rivals' decisions matter.

## A useful classroom rhythm

Introduce the town problem before the formal result. Invite students to predict an outcome and explain their reasoning. Where an activity is linked, run the classroom experiment and use its results to develop the economic idea in the slides. Finish by asking what would change if a price, constraint, incentive, or competitive setting changed.

The question on each module page is a discussion prompt written for this course guide, rather than an additional graded assignment.

## Running the live games

The authoritative workflow is the [Marginal Falls instructor manual](https://marginalfalls.com/instructors/). It describes requesting instructor access, opening a room, sharing the room code, admitting students, starting decisions, and discussing the results.

Instructors should [request access](https://marginalfalls.com/contact/) in advance, using a faculty email address and a public faculty or institutional profile. Students open the same activity and join using the instructor's room code. Some controls and stage names differ across activities; use the activity's own instructions and the current manual.

Keep access codes, recovery passwords, and classroom-session information out of this repository.
